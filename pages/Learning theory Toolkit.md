			- Disability and dysfunction as a result of a system upon a student as opposed to a student's fit within a system.

	- ### Week 2: Skinner [[Skinner The Science of Learning and the Art of Teaching]]
		- **TLDR**
			- School makes kids afraid and is inefficient because teachers don't give prompt feedback.
			- The solution is to make schools less scary and more efficient by using tools that allow for revision.
		- **What does this allow me to see?**
		  This understanding of the classroom calls for much more reinforcement (which in modern times has shown to be quite effective, and has advanced). It allows me to see motivation in learning.
			- Specific - "Is this student struggling from a lack of understanding or fear of a subject?"
		- **What might it obscure?**
		  Problems with the students - this theory highly critiques the teachers and system over the student, who is assumed innocent.
		- **When is this especially useful?**
		  In an aversive environment - shows how a student's treatment would negatively affect learning.
		  In others - shows how a lack of reinforcement would slow learning.
		- **How does it compare to other understandings?**
		  McDermott's understanding shows children have disabilities due to a system - this shows how an entire classroom may have their learning hurt by a system.
		  This understanding seems to see the classroom as a forest.
		- **First Take:**
		  For the most part, seems reasonable. Students need reinforcement that isn't usually given in class, tools as a fix to this problem is rather natural. I can tell Skinner has a bit of resentment of teachers - understandable if he was part of this older school system with birch rods and canes, but harmful to his credibility.
		  ...but a lot of his assumptions seem to be... assumptions. There's a lot he says that feels generalizing or just plain wrong.
		- **A well-designed learning system in his view:**
		  From an abstract perspective, one with immediate and plentiful reinforcement, and positive reinforcement valued over negative reinforcement.
		  More concretely, a system where the teacher teaches the base concepts and the student reinforces their concepts on their own.
